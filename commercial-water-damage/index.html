What we do

Water damage in a commercial building in La Palma

A commercial water loss is water in a building that is not a single family home, from a burst pipe, a failed fixture, a roof leak or a drain backup. We handle four parts of it: water extraction, structural drying, cleanup of wet materials and written moisture records.

We also help you coordinate with your property manager and insurer. We do not repair plumbing, roofs or HVAC, and we do not do electrical work. You hire the trade for that, and we work around them.

Who is involved in a commercial loss

Building owner

Owns the structure, the roof and usually the common plumbing. Often holds the property policy.

Tenant

Occupies a space and may be responsible for contents, improvements and business interruption.

Property manager

Often the first call. May control access, vendors and written notices to the tenant.

Insurer

Decides what is covered under each policy. The owner and tenant may have different ones.

Who calls us and who pays depends on the lease. Ask your lease and your insurer before work starts, and put the decision in writing so there is no dispute later.

Safety steps for the first hour of a commercial loss

Stop the water

Close the nearest shutoff valve or ask the property manager to close the building main.

Mind the electrical

Keep people away from wet outlets, panels and equipment. Shut power to the wet area only if you can do it dry.

Move what you can

Lift paper records, inventory and electronics off the floor if it is safe to do so.

Take pictures

Photograph the water, the source and the damage before anything is moved.

What a restoration crew does in a commercial space

1

Extract

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors and low areas.

2

Assess

Meters and a thermal camera map wet carpet, drywall, ceiling tile and subfloor.

3

Remove

Wet materials that cannot dry in place, such as insulation and ceiling tile, are taken out.

4

Dry and record

Dehumidifiers and air movers run while daily readings go into a written log.

The method follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its crews work to it. For the drying science, see the structural drying page.

Permits and rebuild questions

Drying and cleanup are different from a rebuild. If walls, ceilings or flooring are replaced, permit rules vary, so ask the city building department, which sits with Community Development at City Hall.

For a leased space, the landlord may control the rebuild even when the tenant dealt with the water. Ask your lease who handles it and let the property manager know before any wall is cut.

What changes the cost

We explain scope and price before work starts. The real number comes from seeing the damage and taking readings.

  • Floor area wet and number of rooms affected
  • Water category and how long it sat
  • Amount of carpet, tile, drywall or insulation removed
  • Days of drying equipment and monitoring
  • Access rules and the need to work around occupants

Insurance in a commercial loss

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks often are not. Flood usually needs a separate policy. Check your policy, and ask your insurer whether the owner policy, the tenant policy or both apply.

Our moisture log and photographs support the claim record, but the insurer makes the decision. We do not promise coverage.

Mistakes to avoid after a commercial water loss

  • Waiting for a landlord to respond before stopping the water
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them
  • Letting carpet sit wet for days
  • Assuming the owner policy covers tenant contents
  • Restarting a wet HVAC system or equipment without a check

Common questions

Can I call about commercial water damage at night or on a weekend in La Palma, CA?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays. Tell the dispatcher what happened and what has been done.

Do you fix the broken pipe or roof?

No. We handle extraction, drying and cleanup. A plumber or roofer repairs the source, and we coordinate around them.

Who should call, the tenant or the owner?

It depends on the lease. Ask your lease and your insurer, and tell the property manager at once.

Can you work with my insurer?

Yes. We document moisture readings and damage with dated photographs, and you share them with the insurer. The insurer decides coverage.

What records will I get?

You get a dated moisture log and photographs of the affected areas, which help owners, managers and insurers see what happened.

Do you do the rebuild?

Our focus is the water side. The rebuild scope is decided with the owner, so ask about it when you call.
