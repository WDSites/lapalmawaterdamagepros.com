The area

What Centerpointe is, and why it is not a neighborhood of homes

Centerpointe is the largest office park in La Palma. It sits in the north of the city, in the area the Orange County Business Journal described in 2019 as bounded by Orangethorpe Avenue, Fullerton Creek, Coyote Creek and Valley View Street. Sale announcements describe a campus of about ten buildings and roughly 525,560 square feet, built in 1986 and renovated in 2015 and 2016.

Those same announcements describe a mix of mid-rise offices, single-story research and development buildings and a retail strip. That mix is the reason this page exists. Homes in La Palma are covered on our La Palma home page, while this page speaks to people who manage or work in commercial space.

How water gets into a business park building

Water damage in an office or light industrial suite most often starts inside the building. A supply line fails above a ceiling, a sprinkler head leaks, a roof drain backs up, a restroom fixture overflows or an air conditioning drain line clogs. In a multi-tenant building the water can travel from one suite to the next through shared walls, ceiling space and floor slabs.

Two creeks border the area on the map, but that is a geographic fact and not a flood history. We have not verified the neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status for Centerpointe, so we make no claim about flood risk here. FEMA publishes a flood map service, and a property owner can check a specific address there.

Reading the damage in an office or R&D suite

Stained ceiling tiles

A brown ring or sagging tile usually means water is above the ceiling. Do not push the tile up. It may hold a pocket of water.

Wet carpet or a soft floor edge

Carpet that squishes, or a baseboard that has lifted, points to water under the finish. Moisture meters find how far it has spread.

Musty smell in a closed room

Odor in a storage room or server closet can mean a slow leak behind a wall. It is a reason to open the area up and look.

A tenant next door has a leak

Water that crosses a shared wall is a building problem as well as a suite problem. Tell the property manager in writing.

Safety steps in the first hour in a commercial suite

Stop the water if you can

Ask the property manager or building engineer where the suite shutoff is. Shared risers and sprinkler valves are usually not for tenants to touch.

Keep away from power and wet equipment

Do not stand in water near outlets, copiers or server racks. If water is near electrical gear, call the building engineer or the utility first.

Move what you can, safely

Lift paper files, laptops and inventory onto dry shelves, and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Treat unknown water with caution

Water from a restroom, a drain or the outdoors may be contaminated. Keep people out of the area until it has been assessed.

What we do after the call

We start by asking where the water is, how long it has been there and what the space holds. A technician surveys the area with moisture meters and a thermal camera, then the plan is explained. Extraction comes first, using emergency water removal equipment when there is standing water.

Drying follows. Structural drying and dehumidification sets air movers and dehumidifiers and tracks readings until the materials reach their goals. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard defines water categories and drying classes, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Our commercial water damage restoration page explains what we can and cannot take on in business properties.

What changes the scope of a commercial job

  • Whether the water was clean, gray or contaminated
  • How much floor, ceiling and wall area is wet
  • Ceiling tile, carpet tile, drywall or concrete as the affected material
  • Whether neighboring suites or common areas are involved
  • How quickly the source was shut off
  • Access rules, working hours and building management requirements

Scope and price are explained before work starts. Nobody can give an honest number over the phone, because the moisture readings decide how long drying takes.

Lease, landlord and insurance questions

In multi-tenant space the lease usually says who handles structure and who handles contents and improvements. Read it, tell the property manager in writing and ask your insurer what your policy covers. Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the policy terms, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage.

Photos, dated notes and moisture readings can help document a loss. They do not guarantee a claim outcome, and we do not make coverage decisions. La Palma permit questions for a rebuild go to the Community Development department at City Hall, 7822 Walker Street.

Can I dry a wet commercial floor with fans?

Fans alone rarely dry carpet, pad, drywall or a slab to their drying goals. Contaminated water should never be dried in place with household fans.
