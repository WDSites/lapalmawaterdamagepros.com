The short answer

A slab leak is a pressurized pipe leaking under or inside the concrete

Most homes have supply lines that run through or beneath the foundation, and when one of them pinholes or splits, water has nowhere obvious to go. It pushes up through cracks and joints, soaks the edges of the slab, and wicks into anything touching the concrete.

Many Southern California homes sit on concrete slabs and some do not. Your inspection report or the permit records at the city will tell you which kind you have, and that decides whether this page or the crawl space page fits.

Signs the water is coming from below

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab can warm one patch of tile or carpet. Bare feet notice it first.

Meter keeps moving

With every faucet and appliance off, a meter dial that still turns points to a leak somewhere on your side.

Hissing or running sound

A faint hiss in a quiet house, especially at night, can be pressurized water escaping under the floor.

Damp carpet along a wall

Moisture often shows first at the edge where carpet meets baseboard, then spreads toward the room.

Lifting or buckling floors

Laminate peaks at the seams and wood cups when moisture rises from below. Tile may crack or sound hollow.

Higher water bill

A bill that climbs with no change in habits is a clue, though it never shows where the water is going.

Who fixes the pipe and who fixes the damage

A plumber with leak detection equipment locates the line, then repairs it. Depending on where the leak sits, that can mean opening the slab, or abandoning the line and rerouting new pipe through the walls or attic.

We do not detect or repair pipes. We handle what the water did: extraction, removal of ruined flooring, drying of the subfloor and baseboards, and monitoring of slab moisture. The two trades often overlap, so tell your plumber that a drying crew is coming and ask what areas will be opened.

Slab leak safety steps for the first hour

Close the main

Turn the home shutoff clockwise. If the leak stops, the pipe is on your side of the meter.

Keep power off wet areas

Water near outlets or floor-level appliances is an electrical risk. Switch off that breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes and boxes off wet floors. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs.

Photograph everything

Take pictures of the wet floor, the meter and any visible damage before anything is moved.

Check who supplies your water

The City of La Palma water utility serves most of town and Golden State Water serves portions, so read your bill to know whom to call about the meter.

How we dry a home after a slab leak

1

Extract

Pull standing water with a truck-mounted or portable extractor, working from the low spots outward.

2

Remove

Cut out wet carpet, pad and any flooring that has swelled or delaminated, and take off wet baseboards.

3

Map

Check walls, subfloor and the slab itself with moisture meters and a thermal camera.

4

Dry

Set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, then read the meters every day until the numbers reach the drying goal.

Our process follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. For the equipment side in detail, see structural drying.

Why concrete slows the schedule

Concrete dries far more slowly than carpet or drywall, because water sits deep in the pores and only leaves from the surface. A floor that looks dry on top can still hold moisture that will push up through a new finish.

That is why readings guide the schedule, not the calendar. Flooring installers usually want slab moisture at a stated level before glue-down or floating floors go in, so we hand over the readings and your installer decides.

What changes the cost of slab leak damage

  • How long the leak ran before it was found
  • Whether the water was hot or cold supply and how far it spread
  • Flooring type, since tile, laminate, wood and carpet each need different handling
  • Square footage of wet subfloor and wall base
  • Days of equipment run time needed to bring the slab down to goal
  • Whether the plumber opens the floor, which adds more area to dry

We explain scope before work starts. The real figure comes from seeing the floor and taking readings, so call the 24/7 line and describe what you have found.

Does insurance fit a slab leak?

Policies treat sudden and accidental water damage differently from slow, long-term seepage, and a leak under the slab can be either. Some policies also handle the cost of reaching the pipe separately from the damage the water caused.

Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. The California Department of Insurance publishes a residential property claims guide for consumers. Keep the plumber report, our readings and your photos in one place.

Mistakes to avoid after a slab leak

  • Waiting for the floor to dry on its own while the pipe still leaks
  • Laying new flooring over a slab that was never measured
  • Cutting into the slab yourself without knowing where the lines run
  • Ignoring a high meter reading because the house looks fine
  • Skipping photos before wet carpet comes out

Permit rules vary, so ask Community Development at City Hall whether slab or repipe work needs a permit before your plumber starts. The department sits at 7822 Walker Street in La Palma.

Common questions

Is emergency slab leak water damage available in La Palma, CA?

Yes. The 24/7 dispatch line is open nights, weekends and holidays. Close the main valve if you can, then call and describe the wet areas.

Do you find and fix the leaking pipe?

No. A plumber with leak detection locates and repairs the line. We extract water, remove ruined flooring and dry the structure around it.

How do I know if my home is on a slab?

Check your inspection report or permit records, or ask the city. A home with no crawl space vent or access hatch is often on a slab, but the paperwork confirms it.

How long does a concrete slab take to dry?

Longer than carpet or drywall. Moisture readings guide the schedule, and the number of days depends on how much water reached the concrete.

Can I keep the carpet after a slab leak?

Sometimes, if the water was clean, caught early and the pad can be dried. Wet pad is usually removed, and the carpet decision follows the readings.

Will drying fix the cause of the leak?

No. Drying removes the water that is already there. If the pipe is not repaired, the floor will get wet again.
