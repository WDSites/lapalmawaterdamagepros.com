Why an AC makes water

An air conditioner pulls water out of your house every day

Cooling air also removes humidity, and that moisture collects as condensate on the evaporator coil. It drips into a pan and leaves the house through a small drain line.

When the line clogs or the pan cracks, that water has nowhere to go. It spills into whatever sits below, which is often an attic ceiling, a hall closet or the wall under a mini-split.

What we do and what an HVAC contractor does

We do not repair air conditioners. If water is escaping, switch the system off at the thermostat and call an HVAC contractor to clear the line, fix the pan or replace the part that failed.

Our job starts at the water. We dry the insulation, drywall, framing and flooring it reached, and we repair what cannot be saved. Doing both in the right order keeps the leak from returning to a wet ceiling.

Reading the signs of an AC leak

Stain below an attic unit

A tan or brown ring on a hall or bedroom ceiling often marks an overflowing drain pan. The stain grows each time the system runs.

Wet closet floor

A closet air handler leaks into baseboards and carpet. Look for a swollen baseboard or a musty smell near the door.

Ice on the evaporator coil

Sometimes the coil itself builds up a layer of ice because airflow is poor or refrigerant is low. This is not weather. When the unit stops and the ice melts, a large amount of water arrives at once.

Mini-split wall stain

A drain line behind a wall unit can clog or slope the wrong way. Water then runs down inside the wall and shows as a streak or bubbling paint.

Why summer makes it worse

Cooling systems in Southern California homes run long hours in warm months, so they produce condensate nearly every day. A line that was nearly clogged in spring can fail by midsummer.

A slow drip is the quiet version of the same problem. It may soak attic insulation for weeks before the ceiling shows anything, and a damp attic is harder to dry than a wet floor.

Safety steps in the first hour

Turn the system off

Use the thermostat first, then the breaker or switch at the air handler. A running unit keeps making water.

Mind the ceiling

A stained ceiling may be heavy with trapped water. Stay out from underneath it and put a container below the drip.

Electrical caution

Air handlers have power and control wiring close to the pan. Do not reach into a wet unit, and do not use fixtures that have collected water.

Catch and photograph

Photograph the stain, the pan and the drain line. Dab with towels but leave wet materials in place until they are documented.

How the drying and repair goes

1

Find the wet area

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how far the water traveled.

2

Remove what is ruined

Wet insulation and crumbled drywall come out so the rest can dry.

3

Dry the structure

Air movers and a dehumidifier run until readings return to a dry standard.

4

Repair and finish

Patch, texture and paint the ceiling or wall once it is confirmed dry.

The steps follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Condensate is usually clean water at first, but it turns gray when it sits in a pan or insulation for a long time, and the standard defines those categories.

Permits and the age of your system

If the HVAC contractor recommends replacing the unit, ask who pulls the permit. Permits sit with the Community Development department at La Palma City Hall, and permit rules vary, so ask the city before work begins.

La Palma was incorporated in 1955 as Dairyland, according to the City of La Palma. That says nothing about your own house, so ask your records or inspection report how old your air handler and drain pan are. An old pan is a common reason for a new leak.

Insurance and slow leaks

A sudden pan failure may be treated differently than a drip that ran for months. Check your policy and ask your insurer how they classify gradual leaks, since many policies exclude them.

Record the date you first saw the stain, and keep the HVAC contractor's written finding. Both help show when the leak began.

La Palma Avenue runs through the middle of the city, so it is the easiest reference point when you describe where you live. Give the nearest cross street and say whether the air handler sits in the attic or a closet.

That one detail tells us what tools to bring and whether the HVAC contractor needs to be on site first.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent a repeat

  • Running the system to see if the drip stops
  • Painting over a stain before checking the attic
  • Pouring bleach into a drain line without asking the contractor
  • Skipping seasonal drain line service before summer
  • Ignoring a float switch that has tripped

Common questions

Can I call about AC leak water damage at night or on a weekend in La Palma, CA?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open at all hours. Turn the system off first, then call to plan drying and repair.

Do you fix the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC contractor repairs the unit and clears the drain line. We dry and repair the damage that the water caused.

Why is ice on my AC coil causing a leak?

Ice on the evaporator coil is a mechanical problem, not weather. When it melts, the pan and drain cannot handle the volume and water spills over.

Is the water from an AC dirty?

Condensate starts out fairly clean, but it picks up dust and debris in pans and insulation. We treat surfaces as well as dry them.

How do I know if a leak is slow and hidden?

Look for ceiling spots that grow after hot days, a musty smell or bubbling paint. A moisture meter confirms what the eye cannot.

Will the ceiling need to be replaced?

Only the sections that sagged, crumbled or stayed wet. Firm drywall that dries in place is often repainted instead.
